# NDIS Registration for Sole Traders: A Practical Preparation Guide

Sole traders can apply to become registered NDIS providers, but holding an ABN alone does not make a person registered. The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission assesses the application, required audit and suitability information, then makes the registration decision.

This guide is published by NDIS Provider Registration, an independent commercial preparation service operated by DGTG Pty Ltd. It is general information, not legal advice or a guarantee of approval.

## Step 1: Make sure the sole-trader model is viable

Before applying, write down the supports you intend to deliver, who will receive them, where you will work and what your weekly capacity looks like. Consider how you will manage cancellations, emergencies, leave and records if you are the only worker.

Registration should reflect work you can competently deliver. A broad list of services can add standards, evidence and audit work without creating the capability to deliver those supports.

## Step 2: Check your ABN and business details

The Commission requires applicants to have an Australian Business Number. Check that the ABN is active and that the legal identity used in the application matches the entity that will deliver services, hold insurance and keep participant records.

Prepare your business details, contact information, service locations and key-personnel details before starting the online form. Sole traders still need to answer suitability questions accurately.

## Step 3: Define your service scope

Describe each proposed support in plain language. For example, note the tasks, participant needs, worker skills and boundaries involved. Then compare the description with the Commission’s current registration groups or classes of support.

Do not assume that a trade, personal-care or household service has one universal registration rule. The required pathway depends on the actual support, funding context and current rules. Ask the Commission or an approved quality auditor where your scope is unclear.

## Step 4: Prepare worker screening and operating evidence

The Commission says key personnel and workers in risk-assessed roles need the relevant NDIS worker-screening clearances. Check current status and ensure workers are linked to the organisation in the Worker Screening Database where required.

Build evidence around how your sole-trader service will actually operate. Relevant areas can include participant rights, complaints, incident response, privacy, risk controls, worker induction, service records and continuous improvement. Keep documents proportionate to the business, and make sure you can explain and use them.

## Step 5: Apply, then follow the audit scope

Applications are made through the NDIS Commission Applications Portal. The Commission issues an Initial Scope of Audit describing the relevant registration groups, standards and audit type. The applicant then engages an approved quality auditor independently.

The audit may be verification or certification depending on the supports and services in scope. Ask more than one approved auditor for a written quote using the same scope. Auditor fees are separate from optional preparation assistance.

## A sole-trader readiness checklist

Before beginning the application, confirm you have:

- an active ABN and consistent legal business details;
- a realistic list of supports and delivery locations;
- the skills, qualifications, licences and capacity for the proposed work;
- current worker-screening arrangements for applicable roles;
- service-specific policies and usable records;
- a plan for complaints, incidents, privacy, risk and continuity;
- a folder for application evidence and Commission correspondence; and
- time and budget for an independent audit and follow-up actions.

## Common questions

### Can I register as an NDIS provider if I work alone?

Yes, a sole trader may apply. The Commission still assesses the applicant’s ability and suitability to deliver the proposed supports, and the required audit depends on the scope.

### Is an ABN enough to become registered?

No. An ABN is a requirement, but it does not replace the application, audit or Commission assessment.

### Can a consultant guarantee approval?

No. A consultant may assist with preparation, but the applicant is responsible for truthful and organisation-specific information. The Commission alone decides whether to approve registration.
