# NDIS Provider Registration Portal: Prepare Before You Start the 60-Day Application

The NDIS Commission’s online Applications Portal is where a new applicant submits a provider-registration application. The Commission says an application must be completed within 60 days of starting it; an unfinished application is deleted and must be started again.

The easiest way to avoid deadline pressure is to prepare the business information and evidence first, then begin the portal application when the responsible people can complete it accurately.

## What to prepare before logging in

The Commission’s guidance says applicants need an ABN and information about the organisation, key personnel, proposed supports and services, plus supporting evidence where required. Key personnel and workers in risk-assessed roles also need relevant screening clearances.

Create one working folder with:

- ABN and legal-entity details;
- governance structure, business locations and contact details;
- key-personnel names and required information;
- proposed supports and registration groups or classes of support;
- evidence for the relevant standards and self-assessment questions;
- screening status and organisation links;
- open questions and the person responsible for answering them.

Do not upload generic material simply to fill a field. The application should describe your actual organisation and the services you are prepared to deliver.

## Manage the 60-day window

Before opening a new application, identify the person who will coordinate the work and reserve time for review. Maintain a simple tracker with the date started, outstanding fields, evidence owner and a conservative internal completion deadline well before day 60.

The 60-day period is the portal’s completion window; it is not a promise that the Commission will decide the application within 60 days. Application processing time depends on the organisation, range and complexity of supports and other assessment factors.

## What happens after you submit?

After submission, the Commission issues an Initial Scope of Audit describing the registration groups, service-delivery arrangements, applicable standards and whether verification or certification applies. The applicant is responsible for engaging an approved quality auditor and paying that auditor separately.

When the audit is complete, the auditor sends a recommendation to the Commission. The Commission then reviews the application and suitability information, may request more details, and makes the final registration decision. An audit recommendation is not the same as approval.

## A practical portal workflow

1. Confirm that the ABN and entity details are correct.
2. Agree the proposed services and registration groups with the people responsible for delivery.
3. Check key-personnel and relevant worker-screening information.
4. Prepare organisation-specific evidence and self-assessment responses.
5. Start the application and record its start date.
6. Complete all fields and review declarations for accuracy before submitting.
7. Save the submission acknowledgement and Initial Scope of Audit.
8. Request comparable quotes from approved quality auditors.
9. Track audit actions and Commission requests separately.
10. Wait for the Commission’s written decision before describing the business as registered.

## Common portal questions

### Where do I start a new provider application?

Use the NDIS Commission Applications Portal and choose the option for a new application to be registered as an NDIS provider. Follow the Commission’s current portal instructions.

### What if I cannot finish the application within 60 days?

The Commission says an unfinished application is deleted after the 60-day window and you need to start again. Prepare as much as possible before opening the application and keep a completion tracker.

### Does the 60-day window mean approval takes 60 days?

No. It is the time allowed to complete the application. The Commission says processing time depends on the size and scale of the organisation and the range and complexity of supports and services.

### Can a consultant complete the application for me?

You remain responsible for the truth and accuracy of your application. If you use a consultant, you must understand and be able to explain the material submitted on behalf of your organisation.
