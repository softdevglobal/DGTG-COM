# How to Become an NDIS Provider in Victoria — 2026 Guide

**Published:** 3 September 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 3 September 2026  
**Primary topic:** how to become an NDIS provider in Victoria  
**Publisher:** NDIS Provider Registration, an independent service operated by DGTG Pty Ltd

## Direct answer

To become an NDIS provider in Victoria, you first establish the business and ABN, decide which NDIS supports you intend to deliver, map those supports to the correct registration groups, prepare the policies and evidence required by the NDIS Practice Standards, complete the NDIS Commission application and self-assessment, receive an Initial Scope of Audit, engage an approved quality auditor, complete the required verification or certification audit, and then wait for the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission to make the registration decision.

NDIS provider registration is **national**, not a separate Victorian registration. The Victorian-specific parts sit around the national process — particularly NDIS Worker Screening, Working with Children Checks where relevant, trade or professional licensing, and your local business setup.

For a detailed Australian registration pathway, see the [complete NDIS provider registration guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-provider-registration?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=complete_guide). Victorian providers can also use the [Melbourne registration guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-melbourne?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=melbourne_guide).

## Step 1: Establish the business

Choose the structure you intend to operate under — for example sole trader, company, partnership or incorporated association — and obtain the correct ABN and business details.

Keep the legal entity name, ABN, insurance documents and registration application consistent. Entity mismatches create unnecessary delays later when auditors and the Commission compare your evidence.

## Step 2: Decide what NDIS supports you will provide

Write down the services you actually plan to deliver before selecting registration groups. Examples include:

- household cleaning and domestic assistance;
- gardening and yard maintenance;
- community participation;
- personal care and daily living support;
- home maintenance and home modifications;
- nursing or allied health;
- behaviour support; and
- supported independent living (SIL).

The services you choose determine your registration scope and the audit standards that apply.

## Step 3: Select the correct registration groups

NDIS registration groups describe the supports your organisation is seeking approval to deliver. Applying for unnecessary groups can widen your audit scope, while applying too narrowly can leave intended services outside your registration.

Use the [NDIS registration groups guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-groups?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=registration_groups) when scoping the application.

## Step 4: Determine whether the pathway is verification or certification

The audit pathway is driven by the registration groups in your scope.

Lower-risk supports such as some cleaning, gardening, maintenance, equipment and professional supports may follow a **verification** pathway. Higher-risk supports such as personal care, daily living support, behaviour support and SIL generally require **certification**.

The NDIS Commission confirms the actual pathway through the Initial Scope of Audit. See the [verification vs certification guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/verification-vs-certification?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=audit_pathway).

## Step 5: Complete Victorian worker screening requirements

Victorian workers in risk-assessed roles need the appropriate NDIS Worker Screening clearance. The screening system is administered in Victoria through the Victorian Government, while the screening framework itself is part of the national NDIS safeguarding system.

Providers should maintain a worker-screening register showing the worker, role, clearance status, relevant dates and any other required checks. Where workers provide services to children, Victorian Working with Children Check requirements may also apply.

Official Victorian source: [NDIS Worker Screening Check — Victorian Government](https://www.vic.gov.au/ndis-worker-screening-check).

## Step 6: Prepare policies, procedures and governance evidence

The audit assesses how the organisation meets the applicable NDIS Practice Standards. Common evidence areas include:

- incident management;
- complaints management;
- risk management;
- privacy and information management;
- worker screening and human resources;
- safeguarding;
- service agreements and consent;
- continuous improvement; and
- governance and record keeping.

Documents should reflect the actual organisation and services rather than being generic templates that staff cannot explain or demonstrate.

## Step 7: Complete the self-assessment and application

The application is lodged with the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. Applicants provide entity and key-personnel details, requested registration groups, service locations and a self-assessment against the applicable Practice Standards.

The self-assessment should match the evidence the organisation can actually produce at audit.

## Step 8: Receive the Initial Scope of Audit

After the application is submitted, the Commission issues the Initial Scope of Audit. This document tells the applicant and auditor which standards and audit pathway apply.

The Initial Scope of Audit is authoritative. A consultant or auditor does not decide the registration scope on behalf of the Commission.

## Step 9: Engage an approved quality auditor

The provider chooses an approved quality auditor and pays that auditor directly. Audit fees vary with registration scope, business size, sites and the type of audit, so providers should obtain quotes rather than relying on a generic advertised figure.

NDIS Provider Registration charges a **$500 registration-assistance fee** for its own support work. That fee is separate from independent auditor fees and other business costs.

See the [NDIS registration cost guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-cost?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=cost_guide).

## Step 10: Complete the audit and Commission assessment

The approved quality auditor assesses the provider against the standards in the Initial Scope of Audit and submits its report to the Commission. The Commission then completes its own suitability assessment and makes the registration decision.

No private registration-assistance service or auditor can guarantee approval or a completion date.

## Do all Victorian NDIS providers have to be registered?

No. Registration requirements depend on the supports delivered and how participant plans are managed, not simply on operating in Victoria.

Registration is required for supports where registration is mandatory and to deliver supports to NDIA-managed participants. Some businesses delivering other supports to self-managed or plan-managed participants can operate unregistered, while remaining subject to the NDIS Code of Conduct and other applicable obligations.

Supported independent living changed in 2026: mandatory SIL registration commenced on 1 July 2026 under registration group 0138. Existing providers relying on transition arrangements should check the current Commission guidance directly.

## Can a sole trader become an NDIS provider in Victoria?

Yes. A Victorian sole trader can apply for NDIS provider registration using their own ABN. The provider still needs to meet the standards and evidence requirements that apply to the selected registration groups, including screening, insurance, policies, records, self-assessment and audit requirements.

## Common Victorian provider pathways

### Cleaning and household tasks

Cleaning businesses often have a relatively straightforward scope compared with higher-risk supports, but registration group selection, insurance, worker screening and audit evidence still need to be correct. See the [NDIS registration for cleaners guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-for-cleaners?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=cleaners).

### Support workers and personal care

Support work can range from community participation to higher-risk personal care and daily living supports. The exact service mix determines the registration groups and audit pathway. See the [support worker registration guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-for-support-workers?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=support_workers).

### Trades and home modifications

Electricians, plumbers, builders and maintenance businesses need to consider both NDIS registration scope and their existing Victorian trade or building licensing obligations. See the [NDIS provider registration services](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/services?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=trades).

### Supported independent living

SIL is now a mandatory-registration support and follows a certification pathway. See the [SIL registration guide](https://ndisproviderregistration.au/ndis-registration-for-sil-providers?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=victoria_provider_guide&utm_content=sil).

NDIS Provider Registration is based in Lynbrook, Victoria and provides registration assistance Australia-wide by phone, email and video. Melbourne and South-East Melbourne appointments may be arranged at the client's premises.
