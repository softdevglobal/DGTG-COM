# NDIS Provider Registration Melbourne: Victorian Checklist 2026

**Target keyword:** NDIS provider registration Melbourne  
**Last reviewed:** 30 August 2026

**NDIS provider registration in Melbourne** follows the same national process used throughout Australia. There is no separate Melbourne or Victorian NDIS registration. Applications are made to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, the Commission determines the audit scope, an Approved Quality Auditor completes the required audit and the Commission makes the final registration decision.

What changes for a Victorian provider is the supporting evidence around that national process: Victorian worker screening, Working with Children Checks, trade licensing, professional registration, insurance, service locations and state-based obligations relevant to the supports delivered.

## The national registration process

A Melbourne applicant generally moves through these stages:

1. confirm the supports the business intends to deliver;
2. map those supports to NDIS registration groups;
3. prepare business and key-personnel information;
4. complete the online application and self-assessment;
5. receive an Initial Scope of Audit from the Commission;
6. engage an Approved Quality Auditor;
7. complete a verification or certification audit;
8. respond to any corrective actions or Commission requests;
9. receive the Commission’s registration decision;
10. maintain ongoing compliance after approval.

The Initial Scope of Audit is important because it confirms what the auditor must assess. A provider should not assume its audit pathway merely from its business type.

## National requirements versus Victorian evidence

| Requirement | National or Victorian? | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| NDIS Practice Standards | National | Policies, procedures and operational evidence relevant to the audit scope |
| Registration groups | National | Accurate mapping of intended supports |
| Initial Scope of Audit | National | Commission-issued scope supplied to the auditor |
| NDIS Worker Screening | National scheme administered through Victoria | Current clearances and a worker compliance register |
| Working with Children Check | Victorian | Current check where the role involves children |
| Electrical licensing | Victorian | Current licence and scope appropriate to the work |
| Plumbing and building registration | Victorian | Current registration or licence for the relevant class of work |
| Health-practitioner registration | National through Ahpra | Current registration and professional indemnity evidence |
| Insurance | Commercial and legal requirement | Certificates in the correct applicant entity name |
| Restrictive-practice authorisation | Includes state-based processes | Evidence relevant to the support and Victorian requirements |

A policy pack cannot replace licences, registrations or screening records. The auditor will normally expect the documentary system and the underlying credentials to agree.

## Victorian NDIS Worker Screening

Victoria administers NDIS Worker Screening through the Victorian worker-screening system. Registered providers need to understand which positions are risk-assessed roles and ensure workers performing those roles hold the required clearance or are otherwise working within the applicable rules.

A useful worker compliance register should record:

- worker name;
- role;
- whether the role is risk-assessed;
- screening application or clearance number;
- status;
- issue and expiry dates;
- Working with Children Check where applicable;
- professional or trade credentials;
- induction completion;
- training expiry dates;
- person responsible for monitoring renewal.

Do not wait until the audit to discover that employee, contractor and volunteer records are stored in different places with no central expiry monitoring.

## Common Melbourne provider types

### Support workers and personal-care businesses

These businesses need clear role definitions, screening, induction, training, participant rights, incident management, complaint handling and service-agreement processes. The audit pathway depends on the registration groups selected, not simply on the phrase “support worker”.

### Cleaning and gardening businesses

Household Tasks is commonly associated with cleaning, but gardening, maintenance, transport and personal care should be scoped separately. Evidence may include insurance, chemical and equipment controls, lone-worker procedures, property-access safeguards and participant complaint information.

### Electricians, plumbers and builders

NDIS registration does not replace Victorian occupational licensing. The business must hold the relevant licence or registration for the work it performs. Home-modification providers should also prepare quoting, consent, design, handover, safety and defect-management records.

### Allied health practices

Practitioners need current professional registration where applicable, professional indemnity insurance, scope-of-practice controls, clinical record processes, privacy safeguards, supervision arrangements and evidence that contractors are managed consistently.

### Supported independent living providers

Supported Independent Living involves mandatory registration and a certification audit. It requires a more substantial governance and operational evidence system than a verification-only pathway, including participant safeguards, workforce competence, incident management, emergency planning and separation of service-delivery interests where relevant.

## Melbourne and South-East Melbourne service model

NDIS Provider Registration is based in **Lynbrook VIC 3975** and provides online registration support across Australia. In-person consultations can be arranged at a client’s premises across Melbourne and South-East Melbourne, including areas within Casey, Cardinia, Greater Dandenong and Frankston.

These are service areas, not separate offices. We do not claim shopfronts or branches in Cranbourne, Berwick, Dandenong, Pakenham, Frankston or other suburbs.

A local consultation may be useful for:

- reviewing existing business records;
- mapping staff and contractor roles;
- collecting licence and screening evidence;
- walking through the self-assessment;
- organising an audit evidence room;
- completing a pre-audit readiness review.

The registration and audit rules remain national whether preparation occurs in person or online.

## Melbourne registration checklist

### Business and scope

- [ ] Legal entity and ABN confirmed
- [ ] Business name used consistently
- [ ] Key personnel identified
- [ ] Intended supports listed precisely
- [ ] Registration groups mapped to those supports
- [ ] Victorian service locations and workforce model recorded

### Insurance and credentials

- [ ] Public liability current
- [ ] Professional indemnity current where relevant
- [ ] Workers compensation arranged where required
- [ ] Trade licences current and in scope
- [ ] Ahpra registration current where relevant
- [ ] Vehicle and other service-specific insurance checked

### Workers

- [ ] Risk-assessed roles identified
- [ ] Victorian NDIS Worker Screening evidence recorded
- [ ] Working with Children Checks recorded where relevant
- [ ] Qualifications and licences recorded
- [ ] NDIS Worker Orientation Module evidence retained
- [ ] Induction and role-specific training completed
- [ ] Contractor compliance process documented

- [ ] Incident-management system

## Verification versus certification

The Commission explains that verification is generally used for lower-risk or lower-complexity supports and is completed through a desktop review of evidence. Certification applies to higher-risk or more complex supports and includes a Stage 1 desktop audit and a Stage 2 onsite audit.

If an application contains both verification and certification groups, a certification audit is required. This is why registration groups must be scoped before comparing audit quotes.

## What does registration cost in Melbourne?

The Commission states that there is no fee to register with it, but the provider must pay an Approved Quality Auditor. Audit fees are not set by the Commission and vary according to scope, size, sites and complexity.

A complete budget may include:

- registration preparation;
- policies and evidence systems;
- independent audit fees;
- insurance;
- worker screening;
- professional registrations or trade licences;
- training;
- implementation and record keeping;
- future audit and renewal costs.

NDIS Provider Registration charges a **$500 registration-support service fee**. The independent auditor charges separately and must be paid directly.

## Mistakes Victorian providers should avoid

1. Believing that a Victorian business needs a separate state NDIS registration.
2. Selecting registration groups before defining the service list.
3. Treating an NDIS Worker Screening Check as interchangeable with a Working with Children Check.
4. Assuming NDIS registration replaces a Victorian trade licence.
5. Using insurance under a different legal entity without explanation.
6. Advertising multiple suburb offices that do not exist.
7. Buying generic policies that refer to an organisation unlike the real business.
8. Preparing documents but no registers, forms or implementation evidence.
9. Choosing an auditor before receiving the Commission’s audit scope.
10. Assuming a consultant or auditor can guarantee approval.
