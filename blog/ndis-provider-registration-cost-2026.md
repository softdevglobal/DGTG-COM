# NDIS Provider Registration Cost 2026: What You Actually Pay For

**Target keyword:** NDIS provider registration cost 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 30 August 2026

The **NDIS provider registration cost in 2026** is not one fixed government fee. The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission says there is no cost to register with the Commission, but every applicant must pay an approved quality auditor to complete the audit required for the registration groups in the application. The Commission does not set audit prices, so providers are encouraged to compare quotes from several approved quality auditors.

That distinction matters. A cheap-looking registration package may exclude the audit, insurance, worker screening, implementation work or corrective-action support. A realistic budget separates each cost instead of treating registration as one invoice.

## The five cost categories

### 1. Registration scope and application preparation

Your first cost is the work required to decide which registration groups match the supports you intend to deliver.

This is more important than it sounds. Registration groups determine the standards that apply and whether the audit follows the verification or certification pathway. If the application includes a mix of verification and certification groups, the Commission requires a certification audit.

Applying too broadly can increase audit scope, preparation time and future compliance obligations. Applying too narrowly can leave the business unable to deliver services it planned to offer.

A registration consultant may help with:

- mapping services to registration groups;
- identifying the likely audit pathway;
- preparing the self-assessment;
- organising key-personnel information;
- checking that business, insurance and evidence records use consistent legal details.

NDIS Provider Registration charges a **$500 service fee** for registration preparation support. The approved quality auditor is independent and charges separately.

### 2. Policies, procedures and working evidence

Applicants usually need policies and procedures that reflect the applicable NDIS Practice Standards. The true cost is not just writing documents. The business also needs to operate the processes described in them.

For example, an incident-management policy is incomplete as audit evidence unless the provider also has:

- an incident form;
- a register;
- reporting and escalation rules;
- responsible roles;
- training or induction evidence;
- records showing the process is understood and ready to use.

Generic document packs can create extra work when they refer to committees, job roles, sites or clinical systems that the applicant does not have. The safest approach is to make each document proportionate to the real business.

### 3. The independent quality audit

The audit is the compulsory external cost that varies most.

The Commission explains that:

- **verification audits** apply to lower-risk or lower-complexity supports and use a desktop review of documentary evidence;
- **certification audits** apply to higher-risk or more complex supports and include a Stage 1 desktop audit and a Stage 2 onsite audit;
- the Initial Scope of Audit issued by the Commission confirms the required pathway;
- only an Approved Quality Auditor can assess a provider against the NDIS Practice Standards.

Audit quotes can change based on the registration groups, organisation size, staff numbers, participant numbers, number of sites, travel requirements and the completeness of the evidence.

Before accepting a quote, ask whether it includes:

1. all audit stages;
2. travel and accommodation;
3. technical review;
4. report submission;
5. re-review of corrective actions;
6. any mandatory accreditation or administration charges;
7. GST.

The Commission maintains the current list of approved quality auditors. Obtain comparable written quotes using the same scope so the prices can be assessed fairly.

### 4. Insurance, licences and worker checks

Registration preparation often exposes costs the business would need even without a consultant.

Depending on the supports delivered, these may include:

- public liability insurance;
- professional indemnity insurance;
- workers compensation;
- NDIS Worker Screening Checks for workers in risk-assessed roles;
- Working with Children Checks where applicable;
- professional registrations;
- trade licences;
- first aid, infection control or role-specific training;
- vehicle, equipment or premises compliance.

The legal entity on the insurance certificate should match the entity applying for registration. Mismatched entity names can create avoidable questions during audit.

### 5. Ongoing compliance after approval

Registration is not a once-only document purchase. Ongoing costs can include:

- policy reviews and version control;
- worker screening and qualification renewals;
- incident and complaint management;
- training and supervision records;
- internal audits;
- corrective actions;
- mid-term audits where required;
- renewal audits;
- compliance software or administrative time.

A provider that budgets only for the first audit may be registered but operationally unprepared.

## A practical budgeting method

Build your budget in this order:

| Cost area | How to estimate it |
|---|---|
| Scope and preparation | Fixed consultant quote or internal staff hours |
| Policies and evidence | Confirm what is tailored, implemented and excluded |
| Audit | Obtain multiple AQA quotes using the same scope |
| Insurance and checks | Written quotes for the exact legal entity and workforce |
| Implementation | Training, registers, forms and operating systems |
| Ongoing compliance | Annual allowance plus future audit cycle costs |

Do not choose registration groups merely to reduce audit cost. The application must reflect the supports the business will actually deliver.

## Questions to ask before paying anyone

- Is the audit included or separate?
- Is the provider paying the auditor directly?
- Are the documents personalised to the real service model?
- Does the package include self-assessment support?
- Does it include preparation for auditor interviews?
- Are corrective-action responses included?
- Is any approval guarantee being made?
- What ongoing records must the provider maintain after registration?

No consultant or auditor can guarantee that the Commission will approve an application. The Commission makes the final registration decision.
