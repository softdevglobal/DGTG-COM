# NDIS Registration Consultant Australia: What Good Support Should Include

**Target keyword:** NDIS registration consultant Australia  
**Last reviewed:** 30 August 2026

An **NDIS registration consultant in Australia** is a private adviser who helps an applicant understand the registration pathway, prepare documents and organise evidence before an independent audit. A consultant is not the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, is not an Approved Quality Auditor and cannot approve or guarantee an application.

The value of a consultant should come from reducing avoidable scope errors, documentation gaps and audit rework—not from making unrealistic promises.

## What an NDIS registration consultant should do

A competent consultant should start with the service model, not a document pack.

The first questions should be:

- What supports will the business actually deliver?
- Who will deliver them?
- Will the provider employ staff, use contractors or operate as a sole trader?
- Will services be delivered in participants’ homes, community settings, shared living environments or business premises?
- Which states and territories will the provider operate in?
- Are professional registrations, trade licences or worker checks required?

The answers determine which registration groups are relevant and which NDIS Practice Standards must be addressed.

The Commission’s registration process requires an applicant to specify registration groups, complete a self-assessment against the applicable standards and answer suitability questions about the applicant and key personnel. The consultant should help organise that work while ensuring the applicant understands and approves every answer.

## The consultant, auditor and Commission have different roles

The process works best when the three roles are kept separate.

| Party | Proper role |
|---|---|
| Applicant/provider | Supplies truthful information, approves documents, implements systems and submits the application |
| Registration consultant | Helps scope, prepare, document and organise evidence |
| Approved Quality Auditor | Independently assesses the provider against the applicable NDIS Practice Standards |
| NDIS Commission | Determines the audit scope and makes the registration decision |

Only an Approved Quality Auditor can conduct the required audit. The Commission publishes the current list of approved auditing bodies, and those auditors are independent of the Commission.

A consultant should not select the auditor in a way that compromises independence, claim influence over the outcome or present a commercial relationship as government endorsement.

## What should be included in a consultant package?

A useful package normally covers the following areas.

### Registration scope review

The consultant maps the intended supports to registration groups and explains the likely audit pathway. This should reduce the risk of applying for unnecessary groups or omitting services the business genuinely plans to provide.

### Personalised policies and procedures

Documents should reflect the real organisation. A sole trader should not receive governance documents written for a large organisation with committees, departments and job titles that do not exist.

### Self-assessment support

The application includes a self-assessment against the applicable NDIS Practice Standards. Each answer should be supported by a named document, process or record—not generic statements copied from a template.

### Evidence mapping

The consultant should identify what the auditor may ask to see, including registers, forms, screening records, qualifications, insurance, training records and examples of implemented processes.

### Audit preparation

Preparation should include the likely interview topics, who needs to attend, how evidence is organised and how the provider will explain its systems in plain language.

### Corrective-action support

When an auditor identifies a non-conformity, the provider may need to show more than an amended policy. It may need evidence that the new process has been implemented, communicated and used. Clarify whether this support is included before signing an agreement.

## Warning signs when choosing a consultant

Be cautious when a consultant:

1. guarantees registration approval;
2. claims to be “officially approved” without clear evidence;
3. refuses to separate its fee from the independent auditor’s fee;
4. sells hundreds of generic documents without first understanding the business;
5. tells the applicant to approve self-assessment answers it has not read;
6. promises to lodge false or incomplete information on the applicant’s behalf;
7. uses NDIS or government logos in a way that suggests endorsement;
8. cannot explain what remains the provider’s responsibility;
9. focuses only on passing the first audit and ignores ongoing obligations;
10. pressures the provider to select registration groups it does not intend to use.

The Commission states that application information must be accurate and truthful and may refuse an application containing false or misleading information.

## Questions to ask before engaging anyone

Use these questions to compare providers fairly:

- What exact registration groups are included?
- How do you determine whether verification or certification applies?
- Will every policy be adapted to my workforce and service model?
- What evidence will I need beyond policies?
- Will you help with the self-assessment?
- Is audit preparation included?
- Are auditor fees separate and paid directly to the auditor?
- What happens if the auditor raises a non-conformity?
- Who owns the final documents?
- Will I receive editable copies?
- What ongoing systems must I maintain after approval?
- Are you making any guarantee about the Commission’s decision?

## What the applicant must still do

Even with a consultant, the applicant remains responsible for:

- providing accurate business and key-personnel details;
- deciding which supports the business will deliver;
- obtaining insurance, checks, qualifications and licences;
- reviewing and approving policies;
- operating according to those policies;
- submitting or authorising the application;
- engaging and paying the Approved Quality Auditor;
- attending the audit;
- responding to the Commission;
- maintaining compliance after registration.

No consultant can turn those responsibilities into a completely passive process.
