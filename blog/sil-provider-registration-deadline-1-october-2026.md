# SIL Provider Registration Deadline: 1 October 2026

**Published:** 1 September 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 1 September 2026  
**Primary search topic:** SIL registration deadline, 1 October 2026 NDIS registration, registration group 0138  
**Author/publisher:** NDIS Provider Registration, an independent service operated by DGTG Pty Ltd

## Direct answer

Existing **unregistered providers that were already delivering Supported Independent Living (SIL) before 1 July 2026** must submit a valid NDIS provider-registration application by **1 October 2026** if they intend to keep delivering SIL while the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission assesses the application.

The relevant registration group is **0138 — Assistance with Supported Independent Living**. SIL follows a **certification audit** pathway. Applying by the date is not the same as being approved: an approved quality auditor conducts the independent audit, and the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission makes the registration decision.

This article summarises the public transition guidance. Providers should check the current Commission and NDIS pages linked below because individual circumstances and regulatory guidance can change.

## The two dates do different jobs

| Date | What it means |
|---|---|
| **1 July 2026** | Mandatory registration for SIL commenced, and the new 0138 SIL registration and claiming arrangements took effect. |
| **1 October 2026** | Existing unregistered providers that delivered SIL before 1 July must have applied if they want to continue delivering SIL under the transition arrangements while their application is assessed. |

The 1 October date is an **application deadline for the relevant transition pathway**, not a promise that registration will be completed by then.

## Who the 1 October deadline applies to

The deadline is most relevant to an organisation or sole trader that:

- was already delivering SIL before 1 July 2026;
- was not registered for SIL when mandatory registration commenced; and
- wants to continue delivering SIL after 30 September 2026 while its registration application is assessed.

The Commission’s transition pathway says providers in that situation can continue delivering SIL during the registration process when they apply by 1 October 2026.

A new provider planning to enter the SIL market should not treat this as permission to begin delivering first and apply later. New providers need the registration position required for their circumstances before commencing SIL.

## What should be included in the application scope

The application needs to cover the supports the organisation actually intends to deliver. For SIL, the key group is:

> **0138 — Assistance with Supported Independent Living**

Other supports may require additional registration groups. Examples can include personal activities, behaviour support implementation, community participation or other supports delivered alongside SIL. Adding unnecessary groups can expand the audit; omitting a support the business genuinely delivers can leave the scope incomplete.

## What happens after an application is lodged

The process does not end at submission.

### 1. The Commission issues the audit scope

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission issues an Initial Scope of Audit based on the application. That scope tells the applicant and auditor which standards and modules apply.

### 2. The provider engages an approved quality auditor

The provider chooses an approved quality auditor and pays the auditor directly. Auditor fees are separate from any registration-assistance service fee.

### 3. The provider completes a certification audit

SIL is assessed through certification. The audit commonly examines governance, risk, incident and complaint systems, worker screening and competency, participant documentation, service agreements, support planning, emergency arrangements and SIL-specific operational evidence.

### 4. The Commission assesses suitability and makes the decision

The auditor reports its findings. The Commission separately assesses the provider and key personnel and decides whether registration is approved, refused or subject to conditions.

No consultant or auditor can guarantee approval, change the Commission’s decision or promise a processing date.

## Evidence to organise before the audit

A SIL provider should be able to produce evidence that its written systems operate in practice. Common evidence areas include:

- legal entity, ABN, outlets and key-personnel information;
- worker-screening and qualification records;
- worker orientation, training, competency and supervision records;
- incident, complaint, risk and continuous-improvement registers;
- participant service agreements and consent records;
- participant support plans, risk assessments and emergency plans;
- medication-management and mealtime records where applicable;
- behaviour-support and restrictive-practice records where applicable;
- rosters, handovers and continuity-of-support arrangements;
- house-level emergency, evacuation and disaster arrangements; and
- records showing that workers understand and follow current policies.

## Claiming and invoice implications from 1 October

The NDIS has published claiming guidance for SIL supports. It says unregistered providers that delivered SIL before 1 July 2026 need to have applied for registration if they want to continue under the transition arrangements.

The NDIS also says that from 1 October 2026 plan managers should reject SIL invoices from providers that are neither registered nor have applied to register. Providers that have applied are encouraged to include their registration or application number on invoices.

Providers should confirm the latest claiming requirements directly with the NDIS and make sure their invoices, service agreements and records accurately reflect their status.

## What happens when an existing provider does not apply by 1 October

The Commission’s public guidance says an existing unregistered SIL provider that does not apply by the deadline must stop delivering SIL.

The immediate operational issue is continuity of support. A provider preparing to stop should follow the Commission’s notification and participant-transition guidance, communicate clearly with participants and relevant representatives, and cooperate with transition arrangements so supports do not suddenly disappear.

The provider may still apply for registration later, but it should not continue delivering a support that requires registration while waiting for a future application and audit to be completed.

## Who does what

| Party | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Provider/applicant** | Chooses scope, lodges accurate information, implements compliant systems and produces evidence. |
| **Approved quality auditor** | Independently audits the provider against the applicable NDIS Practice Standards and reports findings. |
| **NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission** | Issues the audit scope, assesses suitability and makes the provider-registration decision. |
| **NDIA/NDIS** | Administers participant plans, payments and claiming guidance; it does not decide provider registration. |
| **NDIS Provider Registration** | Provides private preparation assistance; it does not audit, approve, fast-track or guarantee registration. |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 1 October 2026 the date SIL providers must be fully registered?

No. For an existing unregistered provider within the published transition pathway, it is the date by which a valid application must be submitted to continue delivering SIL while the application is assessed. Approval remains a separate process.

### What registration group applies to SIL?

0138 — Assistance with Supported Independent Living.

### Is a SIL audit verification or certification?

Certification. The audit assesses the provider against the applicable NDIS Practice Standards, including the SIL standards.

### Can an existing unregistered SIL provider keep claiming after applying?

The NDIS claiming guidance allows relevant providers that have applied under the transition arrangements to continue claiming while the application is assessed. Providers should check the current official guidance and include the required registration or application details on invoices.

### Who approves the registration?

The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.

### Can a consultant guarantee registration before the deadline?

No. A consultant can help prepare an application and evidence, but cannot control the audit, the Commission’s assessment or processing time.
