# NDIS Verification Audit Checklist 2026: Evidence to Prepare

**Target keyword:** NDIS verification audit checklist  
**Last reviewed:** 30 August 2026

An **NDIS verification audit checklist** should cover more than policies. Verification is the audit pathway generally used for lower-risk or lower-complexity registration groups. The NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission describes it as a desktop review conducted by an Approved Quality Auditor, using documentary evidence relevant to the provider’s size, scale and registration scope.

The Commission’s Initial Scope of Audit is the controlling document. It confirms the registration groups and audit pathway that apply. This checklist is a preparation tool, not a substitute for that scope or for instructions from the independent auditor.

## 1. Confirm the registration scope before collecting documents

Start with the supports the business will actually deliver. Then compare those services with the registration groups shown in the application and Initial Scope of Audit.

Check that:

- every intended support is represented;
- no speculative or unnecessary registration group remains;
- the provider understands whether every selected group is verification-based;
- the legal entity, ABN and key personnel are correct;
- the business name is used consistently;
- locations and states of operation are accurate;
- the workforce model—employees, contractors or sole trader—is correctly described.

If the application includes a certification registration group, the Commission requires a certification audit rather than a verification-only audit. Do not build a verification evidence pack until the scope is confirmed.

## 2. Business identity and governance records

Prepare a clear folder containing:

- Australian Business Number evidence;
- ASIC or incorporation records where applicable;
- business-name evidence;
- organisation chart or sole-trader responsibility statement;
- key-personnel details;
- position descriptions;
- delegations and decision-making responsibilities;
- conflict-of-interest declaration or register;
- evidence that the person attending the audit can explain the business model.

The names on the application, insurance certificates, policies and contracts should align. An unexplained mismatch between the applicant entity and the insured entity can delay the audit review.

## 3. Insurance checklist

The auditor may request insurance appropriate to the supports and business model. Common evidence includes:

- public liability insurance;
- professional indemnity insurance where relevant;
- workers compensation where staff are employed;
- motor vehicle insurance where transport is delivered;
- property, cyber or other cover where relevant to the risk profile.

Check each certificate for:

- correct legal entity;
- policy number;
- coverage period;
- coverage amount;
- business activities covered;
- current expiry date.

Do not assume that a generic public-liability certificate automatically covers every NDIS service listed in the application.

## 4. Qualifications, licences and professional memberships

For every support that depends on a qualification, licence or professional registration, prepare:

- qualification certificate;
- current professional registration;
- trade licence;
- professional membership where required;
- scope-of-practice statement;
- continuing professional development records;
- supervision arrangements where a worker is provisional, junior or restricted.

Create a simple register with the worker’s name, credential, issuing body, number, issue date, expiry date and person responsible for renewal monitoring.

## 5. Worker screening and workforce evidence

Registered providers must understand which roles are risk-assessed and ensure the required screening arrangements are in place.

Prepare:

- NDIS Worker Screening evidence where applicable;
- Working with Children Check records where services involve children;
- police checks where required by the provider’s risk controls or contracts;
- identity and right-to-work checks;
- signed contracts or engagement agreements;
- position descriptions;
- induction checklist;
- NDIS Worker Orientation Module completion evidence;
- role-specific training records;
- code-of-conduct acknowledgement;
- confidentiality and privacy acknowledgement;
- worker compliance register with expiry monitoring.

For contractors, document who is responsible for verifying credentials, maintaining insurance and managing incidents or complaints arising from their work.

## 6. Core policies and procedures

The exact policy set depends on the registration groups and organisation. Common verification evidence includes policies covering:

- rights and person-centred supports;
- privacy and dignity;
- independence and informed choice;
- violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation and discrimination prevention;
- governance and operational management;
- risk management;
- quality management and continuous improvement;
- information and record management;
- feedback and complaints;
- incident management;
- human resource management;
- continuity of supports;
- emergency and disaster management;
- access to supports;
- service agreements and support planning where relevant;
- infection prevention and workplace safety where relevant.

Every policy should identify:

1. its owner;
2. approval date;
3. review date;
4. version number;
5. responsibilities;
6. the practical steps workers follow;
7. related forms and registers;
8. escalation and reporting requirements.

A policy should describe the organisation that actually exists. Avoid references to boards, committees, clinical teams or offices that the provider does not have.

## 7. Registers and forms

Policies explain what should happen. Registers and forms show how the system will be recorded.

Prepare blank, usable templates for:

- incident report;
- incident register;
- complaint form;
- complaint register;
- risk assessment;
- risk register;
- continuous-improvement register;
- conflict-of-interest register;
- worker compliance register;
- training register;
- policy register;
- document-control register;
- emergency contact and continuity plan;
- participant consent and communication-preference record;
- service agreement;
- privacy or information-access request;
- feedback form.

The forms should match the wording and responsibilities in the policies. For example, if the incident policy requires review within a specified period, the incident form or register should capture the review date and responsible person.

## 8. Evidence that the system has been implemented

A verification audit may occur before the provider has delivered services. Even so, the auditor needs evidence that the systems are ready to operate.

Useful implementation evidence includes:

- signed policy approvals;
- completed worker induction records;
- training attendance records;
- a test entry in each register clearly marked as a test;
- minutes from a risk or readiness review;
- evidence that emergency contacts were checked;
- a mock incident exercise;
- a complaints information sheet ready to give participants;
- a completed internal audit or readiness review;
- corrective actions raised and closed during preparation;
- document version-control records.

Do not invent real incidents, participants or complaints. Use clearly labelled test scenarios where the business has not commenced service delivery.

## 9. Participant-facing documents

Prepare the documents a participant would receive or use, such as:

- service agreement;
- schedule of supports or pricing information;
- privacy notice;
- consent form;
- complaint and feedback information;
- incident and emergency contact information;
- cancellation terms;
- advocacy information;
- easy-read or accessible information where relevant;
- contact details for the NDIS Commission and other external complaint options.

The language should be understandable and should not overstate what the provider is registered, qualified or authorised to deliver.

## 10. Audit-day preparation

Before the desktop review or interview:

- create one indexed evidence folder;
- name files consistently;
- remove duplicate and obsolete versions;
- map each audit requirement to a document and record;
- ensure hyperlinks and attachments open;
- confirm who will answer questions;
- rehearse how the provider will explain its service model;
- prepare a list of any documents still pending and the expected completion date;
- keep the auditor’s request list and Initial Scope of Audit at the front of the pack.

A simple evidence index can use these columns:

| Requirement | Policy or procedure | Supporting record | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incident management | Incident Management Policy | Incident form and register | Director | Ready |
| Complaints | Feedback and Complaints Policy | Complaint form and register | Director | Ready |
| Worker compliance | Human Resources Procedure | Screening and training register | Manager | Ready |
| Risk management | Risk Management Policy | Risk assessment and register | Director | Ready |

## 11. Corrective-action readiness

An auditor may identify a non-conformity or request clarification. Prepare to respond with:

- the issue identified;
- root cause;
- corrective action;
- responsible person;
- target date;
- revised document where needed;
- evidence that the change was implemented;
- verification that the action is effective.

Changing a sentence in a policy is not always enough. The auditor may need evidence that the business process, training or record-keeping also changed.

## Final verification audit checklist

Before submitting the evidence pack, confirm:

- [ ] Initial Scope of Audit reviewed
- [ ] Registration groups confirmed
- [ ] Legal entity and ABN consistent
- [ ] Insurance current and correctly named
- [ ] Qualifications and licences current
- [ ] Worker screening and induction evidence ready
- [ ] Policies approved, versioned and personalised
- [ ] Registers and forms match the policies
- [ ] Participant-facing documents ready
- [ ] Implementation evidence included
- [ ] Evidence index completed
- [ ] Obsolete documents removed
- [ ] Audit interview roles confirmed
- [ ] Corrective-action process understood
